Summer 2026 Maker Camp

Real 3D Modeling Camp

A 5-day-per-week summer camp plan for a smart 10-year-old who already sculpts in the real world and wants to learn real 3D modeling for 3D printing, with vacation protected from May 28 through June 5. Main tool: Blender. Secondary tool later: Fusion for exact functional parts.

Daily Camp Rhythm

Keep sessions short enough to stay fun. Most days should be 60 to 90 minutes. Longer print days can run in the background with parent supervision.

10 minWarm up: open yesterday's file, review the goal, fix one small thing.
20 minSkill lesson: one new tool only.
30 minBuild time: apply the skill to the project.
10 minSave, export, screenshot, or write a camp note.
OptionalSlice or print only when the model is worth testing.

Parent Safety Rules

She can model, choose designs, inspect slicer previews, and keep the print log. Parent controls printer setup, hot parts, filament changes, failed-print removal, blades, sanding, ventilation decisions, and account setup.

ToolRole
BlenderMain learning tool for sculpting, modeling, cleanup, and STL export.
PrusaSlicer or CuraSlicer for turning models into printable files.
FusionAdd later for exact measurements and functional prints.

Camp Rules That Matter

RuleWhy it matters
One new tool per dayBlender gets overwhelming when a beginner tries everything at once.
Big shapes before detailsGood sculpture starts with silhouette and proportion.
Save versionsUse filenames like creature_v01.blend, creature_v02.blend, final_export.stl.
Print small firstPrototype prints reveal weak parts before wasting hours and filament.
Keep a print logEvery failed print becomes useful if she records what happened.
Vacation is protectedMay 28 through June 5 has no required lessons. Optional inspiration notes are fine, but no make-up work is required.
